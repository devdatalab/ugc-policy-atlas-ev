Distributional value

Optional, illustrative weighting of direct-engagement worker benefits by household consumption level.

Expected value model

What this page does

This page starts with the direct-channel worker-benefit present value from the expected-value dashboard. It then tests how the welfare value changes when that same benefit reaches households with different consumption levels.

Gains reaching lower-consumption households receive greater weight. The resulting dollar figure is a consumption-equivalent welfare value.

Evidence and method

Consumption baseline
All-India urban MPCE from the 2023–24 HCES: bottom 40% ₹3,938, middle 40% ₹6,877, top 20% ₹13,351 per person per month. MoSPI HCES 2023–24, Statement 2U

Benefit source
The direct worker-benefit total comes from the expected-value dashboard’s illustrative ADB-based benchmark.

Scope
Direct engagement only. The scenarios state who receives benefits; assigning policy costs to particular groups remains a future extension.

Who receives the fixed benefit?

Choose an explicit incidence assumption. It changes the allocation and the consumption-equivalent value while the direct worker-benefit total stays fixed. This page applies the weighting to direct engagement.

Each split allocates the fixed benefit across three groups of urban residents, ranked by monthly consumption: the bottom 40%, middle 40%, and top 20%.

Population-proportional
Benefit split, bottom / middle / top: 40% / 40% / 20%.
Worker-focused
Benefit split, bottom / middle / top: 60% / 35% / 5%.
Regressive risk
Benefit split, bottom / middle / top: 25% / 45% / 30%.
