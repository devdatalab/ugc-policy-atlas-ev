Distributional value

Optional, illustrative weighting of direct-engagement worker benefits by household consumption level.

Expected value model

What this page asks

The expected-value model gives direct engagement a fixed worker-benefit total. This page shows how the welfare value of that total changes when it reaches households with different consumption levels.

Lower-consumption households receive greater welfare weight. The result is a consumption-equivalent welfare value; household-spending estimates require a separate income-and-spending model.

Evidence and method

Consumption baseline: all-India urban MPCE from the 2022–23 HCES: bottom 40% ₹3,473, middle 40% ₹6,265, top 20% ₹13,132 per person per month. MoSPI HCES 2022–23

Benefit source: direct worker benefits use the main model’s illustrative ADB-based benchmark.

Scope: direct engagement only; beneficiary incidence and cost bearers remain explicit assumptions.

Who receives the fixed benefit?

Choose an explicit incidence assumption. It changes the allocation and the consumption-equivalent value while the direct worker-benefit total stays fixed. This page applies the weighting to direct engagement.

Each split allocates the fixed benefit across three groups of urban residents, ranked by monthly consumption: the bottom 40%, middle 40%, and top 20%.

Population-proportional
Benefit split, bottom / middle / top: 40% / 40% / 20%.
Worker-focused
Benefit split, bottom / middle / top: 60% / 35% / 5%.
Regressive risk
Benefit split, bottom / middle / top: 25% / 45% / 30%.
