Valuation and discounting

Annual benefit per successful city = population × GDP per resident × worker-benefit benchmark × share retained.

No purchasing-power or income-based welfare weights are applied.

The ADB study of transit and land-use reform in Bengaluru supplies the benchmark: 0.82 × 0.15% = 0.123% of city GDP. Only the worker component is used; land appreciation and GDP from migration are excluded. ADB, §6.4

The default benefit-size adjustment retains 25% of the benchmark per successful reform.

Amounts are in constant 2026 USD. Transit is assumed to exist or be funded separately. Include any additional infrastructure or social costs under policy costs.

Other benefit channels
Research and due-diligence costs saved
Estimate projects × hours saved × value of time. Exclude work funded by the grant and savings already counted in policy costs. Not included in the total.
Better design and avoiding harmful policy
The Atlas may improve reforms that would happen anyway. Count the improvement, including avoided losses. Not included in the total.
Earlier reform
If reform would happen later without the Atlas, count only the value of bringing it forward. Shorten the benefit period accordingly.
State or national changes and policy diffusion
One decision can affect many cities. Count each city once, under the appropriate channel. Shared decisions make outcomes correlated.
Private investment and development decisions
Clearer rules may improve site selection and reduce uncertainty. Count savings and other economic benefits, excluding construction spending and benefits already counted above.
